# Work

## What a survey actually is
A hydrographic survey is a measurement campaign. We mount a multibeam echo sounder on a small boat, fix its position with satellite corrections, record the motion of the boat many times a second, and measure the speed of sound through the water column. Then we combine all of it into a grid of depths. Skip any one of those steps and the chart is confidently wrong, which is worse than a blank.

## Datum Line Surveys
The studio has two people: me and Farid Nasser. We founded it in 2021, working from a rented room above a chandler's shop in Fujairah. Our first job was a 14 square kilometre survey for a small marina operator, finished in 19 days with one day lost to fog. We now own one survey launch, a 7.5 metre aluminium boat named Kestrel, and we charter larger vessels when a job needs them.

## The kit
Our main instrument is a 400 kHz multibeam echo sounder. At 20 metres depth it gives us a swath about 100 metres wide and a seabed grid with 25 centimetre cells. For deep or very shallow work we borrow a different sensor. We carry a sound velocity profiler that we lower on a line, and we use it at the start and the middle of each survey day. Sound travels faster in warm water, and in summer the east coast can be 31 degrees at the surface and much colder at 30 metres, so skipping the profile bends every beam.

## What clients ask for
Most of our work falls into three kinds:
- Pre-dredge and post-dredge surveys for harbours and marinas.
- Route surveys for subsea cables and pipelines.
- Wreck and obstruction checks, which are the fun ones.

## A typical day
We leave the quay at 6:30 in the morning because the water is calmest then. We run parallel lines at about 4 knots, which feels slow, and each line is a strip of seabed the width of a football pitch. By 2 in the afternoon the wind has usually come up and we head in. The evening is data cleaning, which is Farid's favourite part and my least favourite.

## Rules I keep
I won't survey in conditions I wouldn't swim in. I don't take on more than three jobs at a time. And every report we send includes a plain-language page for people who don't read bathymetry, because the person who signs the invoice rarely does.
