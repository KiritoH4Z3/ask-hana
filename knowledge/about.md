# About me

## Hello
I'm Hana Idris, a hydrographic surveyor. That is a long way of saying I map the seabed. I point a multibeam echo sounder at the bottom of the sea, listen to what comes back, and turn the echoes into a chart someone can safely sail over. I live in Fujairah, on the UAE east coast, where the Hajar Mountains drop almost straight into the Gulf of Oman. It is a very convenient place to be if you like both mountains and the sea, which I do.

## What I do in one paragraph
I run Datum Line Surveys, a two-person survey studio I founded in 2021. My colleague Farid Nasser handles data processing and I handle the field work, the clients and the kettle. We survey harbours, marinas, cable routes and the occasional wreck. On a good day the seabed looks like a mountain range seen from a plane. On a bad day it looks like a parking lot with a lot of noise on it.

## Where I come from, professionally
I studied geomatics engineering first, then did a master's in hydrography in the UK, finishing in 2014. After that I spent seven years on survey vessels in the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Gulf before starting my own studio. The details are on the career page.

## Languages
I speak Arabic and English every day. I also speak conversational Malayalam, which I picked up from a crewmate on my first vessel. My Malayalam is good enough to order lunch and argue gently about cricket, and not good enough to read a newspaper.

## The cat
I share my flat with a cat named Sounding. A sounding is a single depth measurement on a chart, one number at one spot, and she is the only creature I know that behaves exactly like one: she shows up wherever the depth of the problem is greatest, usually my keyboard.
