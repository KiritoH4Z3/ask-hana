# Food

## The sourdough
My date-and-cardamom sourdough is the thing I bake most, usually on Saturday mornings. My starter is called Lagoon, and it is five years old. I feed it every 12 hours and keep it at room temperature, which on the east coast in summer means it is a bit too happy. The dough is 75 percent hydration.

## The recipe, roughly
For one loaf I use 500 g of bread flour, 375 g of water, 10 g of salt, 100 g of active starter, 120 g of chopped dates and 2 teaspoons of freshly ground cardamom. I fold the dough every 30 minutes for the first 2 hours, then leave it in the fridge for 18 hours. I bake in a cast iron pot at 240 degrees, lid on for 25 minutes and lid off for 20. The loaf weighs about 900 g.

## What went wrong
My first 14 loaves were bricks. Loaf 15 was good enough to share, loaf 16 gave me food poisoning from a bad batch of dates, and I bought a thermometer after that. I keep a baking notebook in the same style as the dive logbook, and so far it has 63 entries.

## Fish and the market
I buy fish at the Fujairah fish market on Friday mornings, and my favourite is hammour, grilled whole with lemon and a little cumin. On field days I eat a cold cheese sandwich on the boat, and I have strong opinions about the sandwich.

## Karak, and a confession
I drink karak, the sweet spiced milk tea, twice a day, and a third time if the day goes badly. I admit that I cannot stand raw okra. I have tried it, many times, in three countries.

## Cooking on the vessel
On the MV Khor Falcon the cook, a quiet man called Sunil, taught me to make a fish curry with coconut and green mango. I make it still, about once a month, and it tastes like the Gulf of Oman at 7 in the evening.

## Rules of the kitchen
No phones at the table. One new recipe a month. Always bake extra, because Farid will turn up.
